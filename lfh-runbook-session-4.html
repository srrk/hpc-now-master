Session IV · Storage Saturation Diagnosis

Distinguishing Busy Devices from Saturated Devices

This session establishes why %util alone is misleading on NVMe systems and why latency plus queue depth form the real saturation signal.

Modern NVMe devices can report 100% utilization while still operating comfortably within capacity. This session teaches how to recognize the difference between healthy heavy load and genuine overload.

Central operational rule.

Busy is not saturated. Rising latency and growing queue depth are the real saturation indicators.
exercise 1

Verify the source of iostat metrics

Kernel disk telemetry counters
cat /proc/diskstats | head

grep nvme /proc/diskstats
Expected interpretation
iostat samples counters from /proc/diskstats.
The kernel maintains cumulative request and queue statistics continuously.
exercise 2

Run iostat with extended statistics

iostat -xh 2

iostat -xh 2 nvme0n1
-x enables extended statistics.
-h enables human-readable units.
2 specifies a two-second sampling interval.
exercise 3

The first-block rule

iostat -xh 2 3
Observe carefully
Block 1:
Since-boot averages → discard.

Blocks 2 and 3:
Current interval telemetry.
The first block may summarize hours or days of historical activity. Always discard it during diagnosis.
exercise 4

Interpret extended iostat fields

iostat -xh 2 nvme0n1
Field Meaning
r/s, w/s IOPS
rkB/s, wkB/s Throughput
rareq-sz Average request size
rrqm/s Merge rate
r_await Completion latency
aqu-sz Queue depth
%util Busy-time percentage
exercise 5

Generate sequential I/O

Sequential streaming write
dd if=/dev/zero of=seq_test bs=1M count=8192
Monitor simultaneously
iostat -xh 2 nvme0n1
Sequential workloads typically show: high throughput, larger request sizes, and elevated merge rates.
exercise 6

Generate random I/O

Random 4K reads
fio --name=randread \
    --filename=rand_test \
    --size=2G \
    --bs=4k \
    --rw=randread \
    --iodepth=32
Random workloads typically produce: small request sizes, low merge rates, and very high IOPS.
exercise 7

Interpret merge-rate metrics

iostat -xh 2 nvme0n1
Fields to observe
rrqm/s
wrqm/s
High merge rates suggest adjacent requests being combined by the block layer, usually indicating sequential access.
exercise 8

Observe r_await and aqu-sz

Generate heavy read load
fio --name=load \
    --filename=loadfile \
    --size=8G \
    --bs=128k \
    --rw=read \
    --iodepth=64
Monitor queue growth
iostat -xh 2 nvme0n1
Rising r_await and aqu-sz together indicate accumulating backlog and developing saturation.
exercise 9

Why %util fails on NVMe

fio --name=nvmebusy \
    --filename=busyfile \
    --size=4G \
    --bs=128k \
    --rw=read \
    --iodepth=16
Observe carefully
%util may remain near 100%
while r_await remains healthy.
A non-empty NVMe queue does not imply saturation. NVMe devices process many requests concurrently.
exercise 10

Busy vs saturated

Condition %util r_await Meaning
Busy High Stable Healthy heavy load
Saturated High Climbing Queue buildup
Saturation means arrivals exceed drain capacity. Latency reveals the transition.
exercise 11

Observe saturation progression

iostat -xh 2 nvme0n1
Typical progression
Early:
%util high, latency stable.

Middle:
Latency rising slowly.

Late:
Latency climbing sharply,
queue depth exploding.
The transition from busy to saturated occurs entirely inside the pinned 100% util region.
exercise 12

The four-combination diagnosis model

%util r_await Diagnosis
Low Low Idle
High Low Busy
High High Saturated
Low High Anomalous
Neither %util nor latency alone is sufficient. Read them together.
exercise 13

Monitor a live workload

Terminal 1
iostat -xh 2 nvme0n1
Terminal 2
fio --name=stream \
    --filename=streamfile \
    --size=10G \
    --rw=read \
    --bs=256k \
    --iodepth=64
Trends across intervals matter more than any individual sample.
exercise 14

The Lustre interpretation boundary

iostat sees only local block devices. It cannot see OSS internal queues or backend storage internals.
The tools change across the storage stack. The latency and queue-depth interpretation does not.

echo "=== START MONITORING ==="

Pinned %util alone does not prove saturation. Rising latency and queue depth do.
